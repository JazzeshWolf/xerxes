Top 5 issues

  1. High bug Call-side candidates are silently thrown away before scoring. sellCandidates sorts puts first, then computeExpiry keeps .slice(0, 24) before conviction is computed. In today’s index snapshot, 108 call strikes (and 11 puts) that passed every filter were never scored. NIFTY 27-Oct and BANKNIFTY 27-Oct show 0 calls.   2. High validation Conviction has not been shown to predict outcomes. The private archive has 3,917 settled stock strikes from the 25-Aug expiry. Win rate is flat across bands (91–98%), and higher conviction does not mean higher return on margin (ρ = −0.04). The 70+ band did go 35/35, but that is one expiry in one calm month. The P(profit) model was under-confident by 5–11 points. xerxes-eod-archive/state/outcomes.jsonl
  3. High bug Failures publish as green, fresh-looking data. If the Upstox token expires or every chain fetch fails, build-stocks still writes a new index.json with count: 0. The publish guard only checks that asOf moved, so it force-pushes an empty screener. Per-stock files that weren’t rebuilt stay published with stale:false. DALBHARAT.json is 34 days old today, and a consumer reading it directly cannot tell. Separately, the EOD archive lost ~13 trading days (26 Aug–14 Sep) while missed-days.json still reads []. build-stocks.mjs:469, 717-826 · stocks.yml:244-260
  4. High design The score can be built from 2 of 7 factors without saying so. Missing factors are dropped and their weight is spread over whatever is left. With no volatility forecast (NSE fallback for indices, or a failed candle fetch for a stock), edge, VRP, cushion and survival all disappear. Conviction then becomes 60% direction + 40% liquidity and still looks like a normal 0–100 score. Today’s NIFTY Oct puts would score 33–55 that way. In a strongly trending market the same path can exceed the index alert bar of 60. analytics.mjs:1322-1331 · build-data.mjs:191-214
  5. Medium bug Liquidity counts the lot size twice. Exchange OI and volume are already in shares (18,768 of 18,769 live OI values are exact multiples of the lot), yet the code multiplies by the lot again. The strike-liquidity factor is nearly saturated (it explains ~2% of score variance), and the name-level liquidity bucket favours large-lot names. Correcting the units would move 29 names out of and 29 into the tradeable cohort that feeds candidates.json. analytics.mjs:692-693, 1304-1305, 584

Next in line: expiry-eve IV-rank and term-structure pollution (33 names at IV rank 100 today vs 8 mid-cycle, and it is applied to next-month trades), tests never run in CI, and no intraday failure alert.

2 · Conviction score: deep dive

Function sellConviction, scripts/analytics.mjs:1220-1442. The same engine scores stocks and indices; indices use INDEX_SELL_OPTS overrides.

How it works, in plain English

For each out-of-the-money strike, Xerxes asks seven questions and gives each one a grade from 0 to 1. Is the premium bigger than what we think the option is really worth? (edge) Is implied vol above our forecast of realised vol? (VRP) Does the trend agree with the side being sold? (direction) How many forecast standard deviations away is the strike? (cushion) How likely is price to touch it before expiry? (survival) Is IV high versus its own history? (IV rank) Can you actually trade it? (liquidity).

The grades are averaged with fixed weights. Missing grades are skipped and the remaining weights are scaled up to total 100%. The average is then multiplied down (or slightly up) by up to six warnings: overnight-gap risk, a recent huge move, IV term structure, put-skew fear, a thin quote, and “most of this premium is tail-risk payment.” The result × 100 is the conviction. 70+ = HIGH, 50–69 = MEDIUM, otherwise LOW.

The weights are hand-set priors. The code says so itself (analytics.mjs:730-732): “trust the band and the factor breakdown, not the last digit.”

2c · Sensitivity: what actually moves the score

Measured over all 2,245 scored strikes in today’s 414 stock-expiry blocks (not just the 48 displayed). Mean conviction 38.4, SD 10.4. Bands: 4 HIGH, 326 MEDIUM, 1,915 LOW.

Nominal weight vs share of score variance

nominal weightshare of conviction variance

Variance share = cov(wᵢsᵢ, conv) / var(conv), which sums to ~93% (the rest comes from the multipliers). Edge and VRP drive the ranking. Direction (15% weight) explains 5% and liquidity (10%) explains 2%. IV rank punches above its weight because it is pinned at 0 or 1 for a third of names.

Factor correlation (sᵢ vs sⱼ)

cushion ↔ survival = 0.98: two formulas for the same thing (distance ÷ forecast vol), 23% of the weight. VRP correlates 0.65 with both because it uses the strike’s IV, and wing IVs sit higher on the smile. So “far from spot” is effectively counted three times, ~41% of nominal weight. Edge is almost independent of everything (≤ 0.21).

Saturation: how often each factor is pinned

pinned at 0in rangepinned at 1

A pinned factor can’t separate candidates. Edge is ≤ 0 for 26% of scored strikes (expected: negative expected value). IV rank sits at 1.0 for 29%, because the 60 cap is low and the min-max rank is taken over only 11–38 points of history.

Missing-data defaults & regime blindness

  • Missing factor → weight redistributed. There is no minimum coverage, so a 2-factor score is shown on the same 0–100 scale. 1322-1331
  • oi=0, volume=0 defaults in the signature. With no quote verdict, liquidity falls back to an older 60/40 formula. 1222, 1319
  • No bootstrap sample (< 60 returns) → Black-Scholes fallback, which the code itself calls “optimistic about far-OTM”. It is flagged empirical:false. All 2,245 scored today were empirical.
  • Expiry week: IV rank and term structure read the current expiry even at DTE 1. Today 33 names are pinned at IVR 100 (vs 8 on 16-Sep), 140/207 are tagged “backwardation”, and both values are stamped onto the next-month candidates too. build-stocks.mjs:380-401
  • IV history mixes tenors. One ATM-IV point per day from whichever expiry is current, so the series has a monthly saw-tooth (IEX: 0.205 → 0.391 overnight into expiry eve).
  • Earnings / results season: not an input. Events appear on the News tab, but the score only sees them indirectly through gap share (trailing 60 bars) and term backwardation.
  • Trend vs range: no regime switch. The direction verdict feeds a capped drift and 15% of the weight.
  • India VIX in stock direction: a market-wide factor (10% of the direction engine) is applied to every stock. build-stocks.mjs:420
  • Calendar vs trading time: t counts calendar days (incl. weekends) while σ is annualised on 252 days. Over a weekend, a short tenor’s variance is overstated (Fri→Tue: 4 calendar vs 2 trading days).
  • No NSE holiday calendar: runs on holidays re-score yesterday’s prices with less time left, so scores drift with no market move.
  • Mark = last trade (LTP), held there by choice; LTP overstates collectable credit by up to 1.21× on gated rows (CLAUDE.md).

2f · Validation: does a higher score mean a better trade?

What exists: the private archive (xerxes-eod-archive) snapshots every name at EOD, and scripts/settle.mjs freezes an outcome row per scored strike when it expires (state/outcomes.jsonl, 4,230 rows). What doesn’t: nothing in Xerxes reads those outcomes. There is no report, no calibration, no weight fitting, and no log of your actual trades. Settlement uses the ~15:45 close, not NSE’s 30-min VWAP. Entry is the first day a strike was scored, not first displayed.

Stocks: mean return on margin by conviction band

3,917 settled strikes, one monthly expiry (25-Aug), 195 names, entries 7–25 Aug. Bars = mean ROI % on the 15% margin proxy; whiskers run from the 5th percentile to the median. The 70+ band (n = 35) did best, but 60–69 did worst (worst −103%). No monotone pattern.

Stocks: win rate by band

A win means the strike expired OTM. The low band still won 90.6%. In a benign month nearly everything wins, so this sample cannot separate good from bad scores.

Calibration of P(profit) (pProfit)

Every bin sits above the diagonal: realised OTM rates beat the forecast by 2–11 points. That is conservative, which is safe for a seller. But it is one month, and a volatile month could flip it.

Rank correlation with outcome (Spearman ρ vs ROI on margin)

Stocks, n ≈ 3,900. The composite score is ≈ 0. Components pull in opposite directions: safety factors (cushion, survival, pProfit) favour far strikes that earn less per rupee of margin, while liquidity (near strikes) correlates +0.55. Edge, the score’s biggest weight, is +0.09. Indices: 313 rows, ρ = −0.06, and only 1 row ≥ 60.

Minimal validation setup (recommendation only)

  1. Freeze the entry. Log each contract the first time it appears on the displayed list (candidates.json), with conviction, every factor s, mark, bid/ask, formula version (a hash of weights and constants) and timestamp. Append-only, one small JSONL per day in the archive repo.
  2. Settle it. Reuse settle.mjs as is. Add the NSE settlement price when it becomes available.
  3. Score the score, weekly. By band: n, win %, mean/median/5th-percentile ROI on margin, max adverse excursion. Spearman ρ(conviction, ROI). pProfit calibration table. Each factor’s ρ with outcome. Post it as a Telegram card and an HTML page.
  4. Decision rule set in advance (like the retired ranker’s ICIR bar): e.g. “trust the ordering only once ρ ≥ 0.10 across ≥ 3 monthly expiries, including one with VIX > 18.”
  5. Log your own trades (Premium Shikari fills and exits) keyed to the same contract ID, so model P&L and real P&L can be compared.

2g · Improvement ideas (ranked)

(i) Leave the formula alone

  1. Weekly outcome report from outcomes.jsonl (validation steps 1–4 above).
  2. Coverage flag on every score: “7/7 factors”, “empirical”, and a visible warning when edge/vrp/cushion/survival are absent. Display only.
  3. Formula version stamp in every JSON, so outcomes can be tied to the weights that produced them.

  5. Intraday failure alert via Telegram (built < 90% of last run, 401 from Upstox, no fresh run in 30 min).
  6. Show raw blend vs final and each multiplier’s cost (partly done in notes).
  7. Log real trades against contract IDs.

(ii) Would change the formula or its inputs

  1. Score before slicing (or slice per side). This fixes T1 without touching weights, but it changes which strikes appear.
  2. Minimum factor coverage: refuse to score, or cap at 49, when the forecast-dependent factors are missing (T2).
  3. Fix liquidity units (drop the extra ×lot) and recalibrate the log thresholds (T3).
  4. Constant-tenor IV (e.g. 30-day interpolated) for IV rank and history. Skip term structure when the near expiry is < 5 DTE (T4).
  5. ATM or smile-adjusted VRP instead of strike IV (T6).
  6. Merge cushion + survival into one distance factor (T5). Do this only after (i)-1 shows what helps.
  7. Trading-day time and an NSE holiday calendar (T11).
  8. Fit or shrink weights once ≥ 3 expiries across regimes are settled.

History loss from force-orphan publishing

stocks-data and gh-pages keep exactly one commit. The EOD archiver was built and is running (private repo, since 11-Aug). It keeps one snapshot per day at ~16:00–22:00 IST (51 MB for 20 days) plus the settled outcomes.

  • Lost for good: ~13 trading days, 26-Aug to 14-Sep (the DB passed GitHub’s 100 MB file limit). No record in missed-days.json. settle.mjs correctly refuses to settle across that gap.
  • Never kept: intraday history. ~30 builds/day are overwritten, so you can’t study how stable a conviction is intraday, when a NEW alert first appeared, or how fast edge decays.
  • Not tied to what you saw: candidates.json is archived only at EOD, so an intraday top-24 that was gone by close is missing.
